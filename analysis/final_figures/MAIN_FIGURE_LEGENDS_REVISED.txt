Fig. 1. Cohorts and analysis design. Eleven public gut-microbiome cohorts contributed to four analysis branches: discovery (seven longitudinal cohorts and one static supportive cohort), independent longitudinal validation (PRJNA1125274), cross-sectional comparison of Control, Trauma and Sepsis samples (PRJNA1010969), and the infection-source extension (CRA002354). Ecological displacement and taxonomic trajectories were assessed separately; their relative reproducibility was not tested formally.

Fig. 2. Cross-cohort longitudinal ecological displacement. Primary early-to-late paired standardized Bray-Curtis displacement changes (solid points) are displayed together with common-anchor sensitivity estimates (open points). Lines connect the primary and common-anchor sensitivity estimates from the same cohort and do not represent confidence intervals. Positive values indicate increasing displacement from the patient-specific baseline and negative values indicate re-convergence. Asterisks denote primary BH-FDR < 0.05. The non-sepsis surgical control showed a non-significant recovery-direction primary contrast but significant re-convergence in the common-anchor sensitivity analysis. Cohort-specific paired sample sizes are reported in Table 2 and Fig. S2.

Fig. 3. Patient-specific trajectories and cross-cohort taxonomic concordance. (a) In the core repeated-sepsis cohort PRJNA691455, points show the 9 patients with paired Day 3 and Day 7 observations; lines connect repeated observations from the same patient. In this paired subset, median Bray-Curtis displacement from the patient-specific baseline increased from 0.725 to 0.908, and the mean paired increase was 0.113. The Day 7 median across all 10 available patients was 0.904, as reported in the Results. (b) Ten patients yielded 45 pairwise patient-to-patient trajectory comparisons (not 45 independent participants). Mean pairwise Euclidean distance was 0.620 (median, 0.554), mean cosine similarity was 0.162, and 35.6% of comparisons had cosine similarity ≤ 0. Points show all pairwise comparisons; the box shows the median and interquartile range, and whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the interquartile range. (c) At the patient level, mean taxonomic trajectory distance was associated with the exploratory ecological injury index (EII; Spearman ρ = 0.733, p = 0.016; permutation p = 0.023) and Simpson-diversity instability (ρ = 0.673, p = 0.033; permutation p = 0.038), but not clearly with latest Bray-Curtis displacement (ρ = 0.236, asymptotic p = 0.511; permutation p = 0.514). The EII is an exploratory ecological-state descriptor, not a clinical prediction score. (d) Cross-cohort concordance of longitudinal taxonomic effects among PRJNA691455, PRJNA851469, and PRJNA516701. Points show pairwise Spearman correlations between shared-taxon CLR effect vectors at genus and family levels. Median pairwise ρ was 0.36 at genus level and 0.08 at family level; among taxa shared across all three cohorts, 36.0% of genera and 31.8% of families changed in the same direction. Panel d shows the primary all-eligible early-to-late-pairs analysis; the common-anchor sensitivity is in Fig. S1. ASV/OTU abundance matrices were not pooled across cohorts.

Fig. 4. Cross-sectional ecological differences between sepsis and severe trauma in PRJNA1010969. The analysis included 17 Control, 18 Trauma and 18 Sepsis samples. (a) Bray-Curtis distance from the healthy-control centroid. Points represent individual samples; boxes show the median and interquartile range, with whiskers extending to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the interquartile range. The 17 Control samples define the reference centroid. Median distance was 0.860 for Sepsis and 0.657 for Trauma (difference, +0.203; BH-FDR = 0.0013). (b) CLR/Aitchison pseudocount sensitivity. PERMANOVA R² ranged from 0.081 to 0.085 across pseudocounts; this range is not a confidence interval. Dispersion heterogeneity was not significant. (c) Correlations between longitudinal and cross-sectional genus-level effects, using 127 overlapping genera. Taxonomic alignment was evident for Sepsis-Control but not Sepsis-Trauma.

Fig. 5. Infection source and longitudinal ecological displacement in CRA002354. The analysis included 26 pulmonary patients with 42 follow-up observations and 15 recorded non-pulmonary patients with 24 follow-up observations. (a) Primary source-by-time interaction for Bray-Curtis displacement (beta = 0.017, 95% CI -0.024 to 0.058; likelihood-ratio p = 0.426). (b) Source-specific slopes per day. (c) Prespecified Bray-Curtis sensitivity analyses. In a-c, points show estimates and horizontal intervals show 95% CIs. The interaction and source-specific slopes were not statistically significant. (d) Baseline source associations. The unrestricted PERMANOVA association was accompanied by dispersion heterogeneity and was not reproduced after restricting baseline sampling to ICU Day 3 or earlier.
