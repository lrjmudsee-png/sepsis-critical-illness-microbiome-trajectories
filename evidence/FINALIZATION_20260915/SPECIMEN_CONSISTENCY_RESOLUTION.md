# Specimen consistency governance audit 2026 09 15

This is a post-hoc sensitivity, not a replacement frozen primary analysis. No patient mapping, sequencing input, ASV matrix or original result was altered. BioSample env_medium and deposited experiment labels agreed for all 160 retained PRJNA516701 Runs: 36 stool and 124 rectal swabs. SubjectID matched the reconstructed patient identifier in all 160. Four primary pairs switched early/late specimen type and a fifth switched between baseline and follow-up.

Restriction to a consistent specimen at every available visit retained 10 of 15 pairs. One uses the first available visit as its frozen anchor; an explicit baseline-anchor restriction leaves 9. Replacing only this cohort's estimate in the original three-cohort REML/Hartung-Knapp synthesis gave Bray Hedges g_z=0.863, CI 0.434-1.293, nominal p=0.0131 (explicit-anchor restriction: g_z=0.865, CI 0.430-1.300). Aitchison remained imprecise (g_z=0.777, CI -0.884-2.437). Excluding the cohort entirely left only two studies, Bray g_z=0.873, CI -1.279-3.026. Thus the same-specimen analysis retains the Bray pattern, but the k=2 exclusion analysis is inconclusive. This does not establish absence of specimen-related bias or biological deterioration.

The original cohort contains both specimen types, confirmed by its accession-linked source paper: https://doi.org/10.1128/mSphere.00358-19. DAY_1/DAY_3/DAY_7 are sampling-interval codes, not verified exact elapsed collection days. Nominal specimen consistency does not control changing stool coating of swabs, feeding, antibiotics, severity or selective follow-up. The secondary family balance remains inconclusive in the specimen-consistent set (9 estimable of 10, mean 1.549, CI -2.146-5.243). All sensitivity tests/intervals are nominal and post-hoc; none replace original primary results.
